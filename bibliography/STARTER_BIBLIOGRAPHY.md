# Starter bibliography — Mu'ta

This is a working bibliography, not a fully audited list. Confirm edition, exact locus and page before final citation.

## Primary and transmitted texts
- al-Waqidi, *Kitab al-Maghazi*, ed. Marsden Jones, 3 vols. (Oxford: Oxford University Press, 1966). Exact Mu'ta passage/pages require collation.
- Ibn Ishaq, *Sira* material transmitted by Ibn Hisham. Select a specific critical Arabic edition and record exact report, isnad and page.
- Theophanes Confessor, *Chronographia*. English translation: Cyril Mango and Roger Scott, *The Chronicle of Theophanes Confessor* (Oxford, 1997). Check Greek text, apparatus and synchronisms, not translation alone.

## Modern scholarship
- David S. Powers, *Muhammad Is Not the Father of Any of Your Men: The Making of the Last Prophet* (Philadelphia: University of Pennsylvania Press, 2009), ch. 5, pp. 72–90. [Publisher](https://www.pennpress.org/9780812221497/muhammad-is-not-the-father-of-any-of-your-men/)
- Stephen J. Shoemaker, *The Death of a Prophet: The End of Muhammad's Life and the Beginnings of Islam* (Philadelphia: University of Pennsylvania Press, 2012), pp. 107–110 and n.165; related discussion p.37. [Publisher](https://www.pennpress.org/9780812243567/the-death-of-a-prophet/)
- Walter E. Kaegi, *Byzantium and the Early Islamic Conquests* (Cambridge, 1992), especially ch. 4. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511470615)
- Lawrence I. Conrad, “Theophanes and the Arabic Historical Tradition: Some Indications of Intercultural Transmission,” *Byzantinische Forschungen* 15 (1990), 1–44. Full article not yet read.
- Robert G. Hoyland, *Theophilus of Edessa's Chronicle and the Circulation of Historical Knowledge in Late Antiquity and Early Islam* (Liverpool, 2011). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/jj.38238823)

I (Amman, 1987), pp. 73–78. [Proceedings PDF](https://centers.ju.edu.jo/ar/Manuscripts/ConferencesPDF/مؤتمر%20الرابع%20%D9%862%D9%851.pdf)

## Interpretive caution
Powers, Shoemaker, Knauf and Kaegi do not offer interchangeable reconstructions. Record exactly which claim each scholar addresses. Criticism of one author's broader thesis does not automatically refute every narrow textual observation in that author's work.
