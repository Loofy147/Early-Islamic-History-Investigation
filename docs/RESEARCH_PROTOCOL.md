# Research protocol

1. **Bound the question.** Specify period, place, actors and claim type.
2. **Freeze assumptions.** List initial assumptions and what evidence could disconfirm each.
3. **Discover sources.** Distinguish primary narrative, document, papyrus, inscription, coin, archaeological evidence, edition, translation and modern study.
4. **Criticize each source.** Record event date, composition date, earliest physical witness, language, genre, audience, author proximity, transmission, dependence, purpose and limits.
5. **Extract atomic claims.** “Source X reports Y” is a claim about a source; “Y happened” is a separate historical claim.
6. **Map dependence.** Distinguish independent witness, dependent report, shared tradition and unknown relationship.
7. **Triangulate.** Compare narratives with documents, inscriptions, coins, papyri, archaeology, geography and logistics. Do not use evidence to prove a claim it cannot measure.
8. **Analyze causation.** Separate stated cause, proximate trigger, structural condition, possible objective, execution and actual outcome.
9. **Assess confidence.** Use high/medium/low/not assessable, explaining why; avoid false numerical precision.
10. **Adversarial review.** Ask an independent reviewer to identify missing sources, source dependence, chronology/geography errors, and unsupported jumps from report to fact.
11. **Publish limits.** State what was read directly, what was only checked through abstracts/extracts, and what remains open.
