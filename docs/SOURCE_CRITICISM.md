# Source criticism checklist

For every source:

1. Identity: author, compiler, transmitter, patron, title, genre, language.
2. Chronology: date of event, composition, earliest manuscript witness, edition and translation date.
3. Proximity: eyewitness, participant, near-contemporary, later compiler, or unknown.
4. Transmission: oral, written, documentary, copied, translated, excerpted or redacted.
5. Dependence: named source, likely intermediary, shared archetype, or unknown.
6. Purpose and audience: historical, legal, devotional, polemical, administrative, commemorative or literary.
7. Political/social location: court, church, city, tribe, scholarly network or other institutional context.
8. Textual condition: variants, lacunae, interpolation, editorial emendation, OCR or translation issues.
9. Expected scope: what would the source reasonably be expected to mention?
10. Evidential reach: what can and cannot be inferred from this source?

Do not assume a contemporary author was an eyewitness, that a late source is invented, or that a source outside the Muslim community is neutral.
