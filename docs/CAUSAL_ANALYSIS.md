# Causal analysis protocol

For each operation, do not collapse the following into one explanation:

1. **Reported motive:** what the source says the decision-makers stated.
2. **Immediate trigger:** the event that may have precipitated action.
3. **Structural conditions:** frontier instability, imperial war, fiscal pressure, succession disputes, climate, trade routes or local rivalries.
4. **Strategic objective:** what the operation may have been intended to achieve.
5. **Operational method:** force composition, route, timing, targets, negotiations, resupply and retreat.
6. **Observed outcome:** battle result, withdrawal, submission, treaty, tax transfer, garrison, appointment or no durable change.
7. **Longer-term consequence:** changes demonstrably connected to the operation.
8. **Unintended effects:** outcomes not clearly intended by decision-makers.

## Required causal tests
- Temporal precedence: did the proposed cause precede the action?
- Mechanism: how could it produce the action?
- Actor linkage: is there evidence that decision-makers knew of the trigger?
- Specificity: does the cause explain why this target, time and method were chosen?
- Alternatives: what else could produce the same action?
- Counterfactual: if the proposed trigger had not occurred, what evidence suggests the action would or would not have happened?
- Outcome separation: do not use later territorial gains as direct proof of the initial objective.

## Output
For each hypothesis, state predictions, supporting evidence, contrary evidence, missing evidence and a confidence assessment. Avoid monocausal explanations unless the evidence is unusually strong.
