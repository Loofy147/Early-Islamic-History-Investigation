# Open questions — Mu'ta case

## Priority 0 — text and chronology
1. What is the exact Greek passage in the critical edition of Theophanes, including apparatus and manuscript variants?
2. How do the chronicle's AM, imperial regnal and ecclesiastical synchronisms relate? Why do modern conversions differ?
3. Does Mothous/Mouchea securely identify Mu'ta, or is this a traditional topographical inference?
4. What is the earliest verifiable Arabic locus for Jumada I, year 8 AH? Are there variant recensions or isnads?

## Priority 1 — source dependence
5. What is Conrad's complete argument about Theophanes and Arabic historical transmission?
6. What does Hoyland's work on circulation of historical knowledge add to the source genealogy?
7. Is any attestation genuinely independent of the Arabic-Islamic tradition?

## Priority 2 — military and geographic setting
8. What Byzantine administrative or military presence existed in the Balqa/Transjordan in the relevant years?
9. What changed after the Sasanian withdrawal from Syria/Palestine?
10. Do routes, water sources, distances and troop claims fit the reported movements?

## Priority 3 — causes and outcomes
11. Can the envoy's mission and killing be verified outside the same narrative tradition?
12. What authority did Shurahbil b. Amr represent, if it can be established?
13. Was the force aimed at punishment, deterrence, a local political network, or wider influence?
14. Is there any evidence of transfer of sovereignty, taxation or administrative arrangements directly after the encounter?

## Rule
An unanswered or unsearched question remains UNKNOWN. Failure to find evidence in a limited search is not proof of non-existence.
