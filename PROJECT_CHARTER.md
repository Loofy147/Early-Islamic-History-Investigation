# Project charter

## Title
A critical reconstruction of the early Islamic polity's military and political expansion, c. 629–661 CE.

## Purpose
Build an auditable, multi-source investigation of military operations, political transitions and their effects. The project presupposes neither pure defence nor pure expansion, neither a single religious motive nor a single central plan. These are hypotheses to be tested case by case.

## Central question
How did military and political processes transfer the authority of the nascent Islamic polity beyond the Arabian Peninsula into territories associated with the Byzantine and Sasanian empires, and what causes, mechanisms and consequences best explain those processes between c. 629 and 661 CE?

## Subquestions
1. What can be established about events, dates, places and participants?
2. Who initiated each escalation, and what independent evidence bears on this?
3. What were the reported motives, proximate triggers and structural conditions?
4. Were decisions central, local, coalition-based or adaptive?
5. When did military contact become durable political, fiscal or administrative control?
6. How did control change: battle, negotiated surrender, treaty, settlement or mixed process?
7. What institutions persisted, and what changed?
8. How did urban and rural populations, elites and religious communities experience these transitions?
9. Which competing explanations remain viable, and what evidence could falsify each?

## Scope
Core period: 629–661 CE. Earlier late-antique context and later consequences may be included only when explicitly justified. Core geography: Arabia, southern Syria/Palestine, Iraq, Egypt, Iran and adjacent regions directly connected to a case.

## Principles
- No conclusion is fixed in advance.
- No source is automatically authoritative or neutral.
- Do not infer intention from outcome alone.
- Do not treat a reported motive as a complete causal explanation.
- Absence, silence and contradiction are distinct.
- Do not generalize from one campaign to all campaigns.
- Separate religious expansion, political sovereignty, military control, fiscal administration, migration and demographic change.

## Deliverables
Source registry; claim ledger; event register; hypothesis register; case studies; uncertainty-aware chronology; comparative analysis; revision and decision logs.

## Success criterion
An independent researcher can trace every material conclusion to a source and locus, inspect contrary evidence and uncertainty, and reassess the conclusion without relying on the author's authority. The goal is not to prove “conquest” or “defence”, but to improve historical explanation.
