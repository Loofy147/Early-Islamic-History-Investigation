# CASE-006 — Campaigns in Syria and Palestine

**Status:** NOT STARTED — discovery only.  
**Scope:** Early military and political transition in Byzantine Syria/Palestine, approximately 634–640 CE; precise chronology and event sequences must be reconstructed rather than assumed.

## Research question
Through which military, diplomatic and administrative processes did Byzantine authority in Syria and Palestine give way to the authority of the early Islamic polity?

## Questions
1. What are the earliest attestations for Dathin, Ajnadayn, Fahl, Yarmuk and the fall of major cities?
2. Which dates, locations and battle descriptions are independently supported?
3. How did imperial field forces, provincial commanders, Arab federates, local elites and religious communities interact?
4. Which towns resisted, negotiated, surrendered or changed hands more than once?
5. What do treaties actually provide for: taxes, property, churches, security, military service and local administration?
6. How rapidly did sovereignty, taxation and garrison control change?

## Required cautions
Do not collapse all Syrian operations into one invasion or one battle. Distinguish battle narratives from the terms of urban capitulation. Do not infer local welcome or rejection from a single community's text.

## Next work
Compare Arabic, Greek, Syriac and Armenian narratives; study the Persian occupation and Byzantine restoration; integrate numismatic, papyrological, epigraphic and archaeological evidence; reconstruct the campaign sequence with uncertainty ranges.
