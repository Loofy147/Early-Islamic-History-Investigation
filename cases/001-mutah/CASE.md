# CASE-001 — Mu'ta / Mothous

**Status:** OPEN — disputed chronology, disputed source independence.  
**Conventional date in Islamic narrative:** Jumada I, 8 AH / 629 CE.  
**Alternative chronology:** Theophanes' Mothous notice is placed in a chronology associated by modern interpreters with Abu Bakr's first year. Exact conversion and synchronisms remain to be checked against the Greek text and critical apparatus.  
**Location:** Mu'ta / Balqa in southern Transjordan is the usual identification; the identification of Greek Mothous/Mouchea needs independent topographical testing.

## Research question
What minimum historical reconstruction can be defended concerning the engagement associated with Mu'ta: its date, location, participants, cause, military character and consequences?

## Keep separate
1. A narrative tradition about an expedition/engagement.
2. A report that an envoy named al-Harith b. Umayr was killed.
3. A causal claim that the killing triggered the expedition.
4. The identification of the opposing force as imperial Byzantine, regional units, Arab client/allied groups, or a combination.
5. The date of the engagement.
6. The military outcome and the meaning of Khalid's withdrawal.
7. Any durable transfer of sovereignty or political control.

## Main source problem
David S. Powers identifies al-Waqidi's *Kitab al-Maghazi* as the earliest extant detailed Islamic account located in his comparison. Al-Waqidi assembles 29 reports and places the event within a sequence suggesting year 8 AH, but does not explicitly give month and year in the battle account. Ibn Ishaq material transmitted by Ibn Hisham includes a dated notice associated with Jumada I, year 8 AH. These are reports about the shape of the surviving tradition; direct collation of the Arabic editions is still required.

Theophanes (d. 818) includes a notice about an engagement at Mothous. Powers and Shoemaker discuss the possibility that it represents an independent Greek/Syriac witness or a report transmitted from an Arabic-Islamic tradition. Theophanes' chronology differs from the conventional Sira dating as interpreted by modern scholars. The Greek passage, synchronisms, textual variants and source genealogy must be verified before drawing a conclusion.

## Competing hypotheses
- **H-MUT-1 — Retaliation:** The expedition was primarily triggered by the killing of an envoy.
- **H-MUT-2 — Frontier security/deterrence:** It sought to protect communication routes or deter local authorities and allied groups.
- **H-MUT-3 — Political expansion:** It aimed to extend the nascent polity's influence/authority into the southern Levant.
- **H-MUT-4 — Local coalition clash:** The encounter was mainly between local Arab groups associated with the Byzantine frontier, not a major regular imperial field army.
- **H-MUT-5 — Chronological conflation/re-dating:** Different event memories or source chronologies were later harmonized into one battle narrative.

These hypotheses can overlap; they are not mutually exclusive unless a specific test makes them so.

## Current provisional position
The existence of a developed Islamic narrative is established as a fact about the later literary tradition. The historicity, exact date, force composition, source independence and causal explanation of the narrated engagement remain open or disputed. No evidence currently reviewed establishes that this engagement itself transferred sovereignty over a territory.
