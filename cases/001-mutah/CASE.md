# CASE-001 — Mu'ta / Mothous

**Status:** OPEN — disputed chronology; uncertain place identification; disputed source dependence.  
**Conventional Islamic narrative date:** Jumada I, 8 AH / 629 CE.  
**Theophanes passage:** AM 6123, labelled AD 630/1 in the Mango–Scott translation; it names Abu Bakr (Aboubacharos) as leader of the Arabs in his first year. It narrates an engagement at Mothous, not explicitly at Mu'ta.  
**Location:** Mu'ta/Balqa in southern Transjordan is the standard Islamic narrative location. The identification of Theophanes' Mothous with Mu'ta is not established: the translation's note says the identification is unclear and records alternatives proposed by scholars (Ma'ab, Khirbat al-Mahna, Mu'an).

## Research question
What minimum historical reconstruction can be defended concerning (a) the Islamic narrative of an expedition to Mu'ta and (b) the separate or potentially related engagement at Mothous in Theophanes?

## Mandatory distinction
The case must not assume that the following refer to one event:
1. The Islamic narrative of an expedition to Mu'ta, conventionally dated to 629.
2. Theophanes' narrative under AM 6123 (AD 630/1) of an engagement at Mothous.
3. Theophanes' nearby mention of a village Mouchea where the vicarius Theodore was stationed.
4. The editor's footnote connecting the Islamic tradition of Mu'ta to 629.
5. The possibility that Mothous and Mu'ta are identical, distinct, or conflated in later interpretation.

## Islamic narrative
David S. Powers identifies al-Waqidi's *Kitab al-Maghazi* as the earliest extant detailed Islamic account located in his comparison. Al-Waqidi assembles reports and places the engagement within a sequence suggesting year 8 AH, but does not explicitly give month and year in the battle account. Ibn Ishaq material transmitted by Ibn Hisham includes a dated notice associated with Jumada I, year 8 AH, according to Powers. The Arabic editions, isnads and variants still require direct collation.

## Theophanes text — exact distinctions
In the Mango–Scott translation, under AM 6123 [AD 630/1], the chronicle states:
- Herakleios is in his 22nd year; Aboubacharos, leader of the Arabs, is in his first year.
- Mouamed, “who had died earlier,” had appointed four emirs to fight Christian Arabs.
- The force came before a village called Mouchea, where the vicarius Theodore was stationed.
- Theodore learned of the intended attack and struck them at a village called Mothous; three emirs and most of the force were killed; one emir named Chaled escaped.
- The passage then describes neighbouring Arabs receiving imperial payments for guarding desert approaches and a wage dispute leading to defection.

The accompanying editorial footnotes require careful reading:
- Note 2 says the traditional Arab expedition to Mu'ta was dated 629, before Muhammad's death.
- Note 3 explicitly says the identification of Mothous is unclear and lists alternatives proposed by modern scholars: Ma'ab (de Goeje), Khirbat al-Mahna (Musil), and Mu'an (Krikov).
- Note 4 says the Greek construction about the day of sacrifice is ambiguous and discusses different interpretations.
- The printed chronology labels the entry AM 6123 [AD 630/1], while the same entry has Aboubacharos in his first year and says Muhammad had died earlier; the note identifies Muhammad's death as 632. The chronicle's synchronisms therefore need a dedicated audit rather than being used as a simple exact date for Mu'ta.

## Source-dependence issue
Theophanes is a ninth-century chronicle, not a contemporary witness. Even if Mothous were geographically identical with Mu'ta, source independence would remain unresolved. Conrad's work on intercultural transmission is central. A Greek-language witness does not become independent merely by being Greek.

## Competing hypotheses
- **H-MUT-1 — Retaliation:** The Islamic expedition was primarily triggered by the reported killing of an envoy.
- **H-MUT-2 — Frontier security/deterrence:** It sought to protect routes or deter local authorities and allied groups.
- **H-MUT-3 — Political expansion:** It aimed to extend the nascent polity's influence/authority into the southern Levant.
- **H-MUT-4 — Local coalition clash:** The opposing force was mainly local Arab groups associated with the Byzantine frontier, not a major regular imperial field army.
- **H-MUT-5 — Separate or conflated events:** The Mu'ta narrative and Theophanes' Mothous episode may be distinct incidents that later scholarship or translation notes have connected.
- **H-MUT-6 — Chronological re-dating:** One or both traditions may preserve a displaced chronology or later synchronisation.

These hypotheses may overlap and are not mutually exclusive unless a specific test makes them so.

## Provisional position
The existence of a detailed Islamic narrative is established as a fact about the literary tradition. Theophanes' text independently establishes that his chronicle narrates an engagement at Mothous within AM 6123; it does not yet establish that this was the same event as the Islamic Mu'ta expedition. Exact chronology, site identification, source dependence, force composition, causation and the historicity of details remain open. No evidence reviewed establishes a territorial sovereignty transfer as a direct outcome of the Mu'ta engagement.
