# CASE-008 — Sasanian Iran and the collapse of imperial authority

**Status:** NOT STARTED — discovery only.  
**Scope:** Campaigns and political transitions in Sasanian territories, approximately 633–651 CE, with earlier context for the late Byzantine–Sasanian war and succession crisis.

## Research question
How did Sasanian imperial authority collapse or transform under military pressure, and how did early Islamic forces and local elites establish new forms of political and fiscal control?

## Questions
1. What was the condition of the Sasanian state after the wars with Byzantium and the succession crisis?
2. Which campaigns and battles are securely dated and geographically located?
3. Which regions changed hands through battle, treaty, local defection or fragmented authority?
4. What roles did marzbans, dihqans, provincial elites and religious institutions play?
5. How did taxation, landholding, coinage, military service and administration change?
6. When did regional transitions occur, and did they proceed at different speeds?

## Required cautions
Do not describe the whole Iranian plateau as falling in one event. Separate the end of the central monarchy from local political submission and administrative integration. Distinguish Arab military presence from the later demographic and linguistic Arabization of different regions.

## Next work
Compare Arabic accounts with Armenian, Syriac, Greek and Middle Persian evidence; use coin sequences, seals, papyri and archaeological material; map regional chronologies rather than assuming a single conquest date.
