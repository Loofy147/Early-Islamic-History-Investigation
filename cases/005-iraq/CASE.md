# CASE-005 — Early campaigns in Iraq

**Status:** NOT STARTED — discovery only.  
**Scope:** Initial operations against territories under Sasanian authority and associated Arab political networks, approximately 633–638 CE; exact dates vary by source tradition.

## Research question
How did armed operations in Iraq begin and develop into durable political control, and what roles did central decisions, local coalitions, Sasanian military disruption, fiscal interests and negotiated settlements play?

## Questions
1. What is the earliest defensible sequence of operations and commanders?
2. How did the recent Byzantine–Sasanian war and Sasanian succession crisis affect regional capacity?
3. Which local Arab groups and settled communities participated, resisted, negotiated or remained neutral?
4. Which cities fell by battle, capitulation, treaty or later administrative transfer?
5. How did tribute, taxation, land tenure and garrison arrangements change?
6. Did military objectives change during the campaign?

## Required cautions
Do not treat Sasanian collapse as a single battle or presume all operations were planned from the outset. Separate regional alliances from religious identity; distinguish reports of battle from terms of settlement and later governance.

## Next work
Create an event-by-event chronology with source dependencies; compare Arabic narratives with Syriac, Armenian and Persian evidence; audit treaty texts and fiscal arrangements; map routes and logistical constraints.
