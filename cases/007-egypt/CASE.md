# CASE-007 — The campaign and political transition in Egypt

**Status:** NOT STARTED — discovery only.  
**Scope:** Operations associated with the early Islamic entry into Egypt and the transition from Byzantine to early Islamic authority, approximately 639–642 CE; dates and phases require independent verification.

## Research question
How did military operations, negotiation, provincial administration, taxation and local political conditions combine in the transfer of authority in Egypt?

## Questions
1. What evidence supports the timing, force size, route and command structure?
2. What role did Byzantine imperial defense and the local civil/administrative apparatus play?
3. What treaties or agreements are attested, and in which textual traditions?
4. How did tax liabilities, land assessment, grain supply and property rights change?
5. What can papyri and documentary evidence establish that narrative sources cannot?
6. How did different local communities experience the transition?

## Required cautions
Do not infer a uniform Egyptian response. Separate military conquest from administrative continuity, fiscal change, religious policy and later demographic transformation. Treat treaty texts as transmitted documents requiring textual criticism.

## Next work
Prioritize Greek, Coptic and Arabic papyri and inscriptions; compare Greek/Syriac/Arabic chronicles; distinguish contemporary documents from later treaty recensions; build a district-level chronology where evidence permits.
